# Schwartz Basic Human Values — Scoring Guide

Ten universal values from Shalom Schwartz's theory of basic human values. Score each on –1 to +1.

---

## The values and their motivational focus

### Universalism
**Core goal**: Understanding, tolerance, appreciation, and protection for the welfare of all people and nature.

**+1 signals in politics**: Climate action (protecting nature for all), universal healthcare, international humanitarian aid, opposing discrimination of any kind, global governance, indigenous rights, refugee protection.

**–1 signals**: Explicit nationalism that excludes outsiders; actions that harm environment for gain; opposition to international cooperation; policies that benefit in-group at expense of out-group or nature.

**Distinguish from benevolence**: Universalism extends to *all people and nature*; benevolence focuses on *close others and community*. A candidate who helps their constituents but ignores global suffering scores high on benevolence but not universalism.

---

### Benevolence
**Core goal**: Preserving and enhancing the welfare of close people and one's community.

**+1 signals**: Community investment, healthcare access for constituents, public school funding, local job creation, social services, care for veterans and seniors, public safety investment.

**–1 signals**: Explicit indifference to community welfare; cutting social services with no alternative; prioritizing abstract ideology over concrete human welfare.

**Note**: Most politicians score positive on benevolence — it's the baseline of claiming to care about constituents. What differentiates candidates is the *scope* of who they include and what mechanisms they support.

---

### Tradition
**Core goal**: Respect for, commitment to, and acceptance of the customs and ideas of one's culture and religion.

**+1 signals**: Defense of existing institutions; religious values in governance; skepticism of rapid change; respect for cultural heritage; pro-family in traditional sense; deference to established norms.

**–1 signals**: Active dismantling of traditions; secular governance explicitly over religious values; challenging established cultural norms as progress; repealing long-standing laws.

**Note**: Tradition is not inherently conservative or progressive — it depends on what traditions are being preserved or challenged. Repealing a 1931 abortion ban is anti-tradition; defending it is pro-tradition.

---

### Conformity
**Core goal**: Restraint of actions, inclinations, and impulses that might harm others or violate social expectations and norms.

**+1 signals**: Rule of law emphasis; institutional process adherence; "work within the system" orientation; deference to democratic norms; supporting social order; opposing norm violations even by allies.

**–1 signals**: Disrupting institutional norms even for good ends; norm violation as strategy; "the ends justify the means"; executive overreach in any direction.

**Distinguish from tradition**: Conformity is about *current social rules and expectations*; tradition is about *historical customs*. You can be high-conformity and reformist (working through legitimate channels to change the law) or high-tradition and anti-conformity (respecting old ways even when they conflict with current law).

---

### Security
**Core goal**: Safety, harmony, and stability of society, of relationships, and of self.

**+1 signals**: National defense investment; border security; law enforcement funding; emergency preparedness; stability of benefits (Social Security, Medicare); food and housing security programs; public health infrastructure.

**–1 signals**: Destabilizing social institutions; defunding security-related agencies; promoting social disruption as a tactic; ignoring public safety for ideological reasons.

**Note**: Security spans left and right. A progressive who wants guaranteed healthcare and housing as security signals scores positive. A conservative who wants strong military and police signals scores positive. The question is which *type* of security and for *whom*.

---

### Power
**Core goal**: Social status, prestige, and control over people and resources.

**+1 signals**: Alignment with concentrated wealth; deference to business elites and their interests; building personal political power; accepting large donor money and behaving accordingly; supporting strong executive authority; favoring wealthy/corporate interests in policy.

**–1 signals**: Anti-corporate orientation; campaign finance reform advocacy; rejecting PAC money as values statement; challenging institutional power structures; supporting regulation of concentrated industries; trust-busting orientation.

**Note**: Power is one of the few Schwartz values where negative scores are relatively common for politicians who explicitly build their identity around challenging power. El-Sayed (no PAC money, anti-corporate) and AOC-type politicians score negative. Rogers-type politicians who align with donor class score positive.

---

### Achievement
**Core goal**: Personal success through demonstrating competence according to social standards.

**+1 signals**: Meritocracy emphasis; individual career advancement; workforce development; skills training; entrepreneurship support; economic mobility through personal effort; "pull yourself up" orientation.

**–1 signals**: Explicit rejection of meritocracy as a myth; structural analysis of success (emphasizing systemic barriers over individual effort).

**Note**: Most politicians score modestly positive here — claiming to support opportunity is universal. What differentiates is whether they emphasize individual effort or structural change as the mechanism.

---

### Hedonism
**Core goal**: Pleasure and sensuous gratification for oneself.

**+1 signals**: Personal freedom and lifestyle liberty emphasis; recreational cannabis legalization as liberty issue; explicit libertarian personal freedom framing; "government out of personal choices" orientation.

**0 or –1 signals**: Restriction of personal pleasure choices; social conservatism around personal behavior; prohibition-oriented policies.

**Note**: This is often the lowest-scoring value for politicians generally, who rarely frame policy around personal pleasure. It shows up most in libertarian-leaning candidates and some progressives on lifestyle freedom issues.

---

### Stimulation
**Core goal**: Excitement, novelty, and challenge in life.

**+1 signals**: Disruption and transformation as explicit goals; "new generation of leaders" rhetoric; structural reform advocacy; innovation emphasis; challenging the status quo as identity; tech optimism; radical policy proposals.

**–1 signals**: Explicit conservatism in the sense of preserving the existing order; "if it ain't broke" orientation; defending existing institutions against change.

**Note**: Progressive and libertarian reformers often score high. Establishment figures of both parties score lower. Anti-establishment candidates of any ideology score higher.

---

### Self-Direction
**Core goal**: Independent thought and action — freedom to choose, create, explore.

**+1 signals**: Civil liberties emphasis; free speech absolutism; anti-surveillance; anti-regulation (individual liberty); reproductive rights as bodily autonomy; LGBTQ+ rights as personal freedom; school choice as parental control; gun rights as individual liberty.

**–1 signals**: Surveillance expansion; restricting reproductive choice; limiting LGBTQ+ rights; government control over personal decisions; mandatory national service.

**Note**: This value crosses ideological lines strongly — conservatives invoke it for gun rights and school choice; progressives invoke it for reproductive rights and LGBTQ+ freedoms. Score based on the overall pattern across issues, not just one domain.

---

## The circular model

Schwartz values form a circle, with adjacent values compatible and opposing values in tension:

```
       Self-Direction ←→ Stimulation
      ↗                              ↘
Universalism                        Hedonism
    ↑                                    ↓
Benevolence                         Achievement
    ↑                                    ↓
Tradition                           Power
      ↘                              ↗
       Conformity ←→ Security
```

Candidates who score very high on **universalism** tend to score lower on **power** (they're opposites). Candidates high on **tradition** tend to score lower on **self-direction**. These tensions are informative — when a candidate defies the expected pattern, it's worth noting why.
