## What is Ballot Builder?

Ballot Builder is a nonpartisan tool that helps you prepare for elections based on your own values — not party labels, endorsements, or someone else's opinion. You take a short assessment, and we match you with the candidates and ballot measures that best align with what you actually care about.

This is a prototype being evaluated for accuracy, fairness, and usefulness. It is not an official voter guide.

---

## How It Works

### Step 1: You tell us what matters to you

The Civic Blueprint Assessment is a 5-minute quiz covering 15 policy topics across five areas: Economy, Healthcare, Housing, Justice, and Climate. For each topic, you choose from concrete policy positions — not vague labels like "liberal" or "conservative."

You also tell us how much each topic matters to you. If you care deeply about climate but don't have strong feelings about zoning, the system weights your recommendations accordingly.

All of your answers stay on your device. We don't send them to a server, and we don't track them.

### Step 2: We score the candidates

Every candidate on your ballot is independently scored on the same 15 policy topics you answered. These scores come from public records, not assumptions.

Our evidence sources, ranked by how much we trust them:

| Priority | Source | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **Highest** | Voting records | How they actually voted on specific bills |
| **Highest** | Interest group scorecards | Numerical ratings from organizations like the League of Conservation Voters, NRA, Chamber of Commerce, AFL-CIO |
| **Medium** | Campaign website | The candidate's own stated positions on their issues page |
| **Medium** | Interviews | Answers to policy questions in media interviews, editorial boards, or voter guides |
| **Lower** | News coverage | Reporting on their positions |
| **Lower** | Endorsements | Which organizations support or oppose them |

We do not score candidates based on party affiliation. A Republican who voted for gun safety legislation gets a different firearms score than one who didn't — because we score the person, not the party.

When a candidate's voting record contradicts what they say on the campaign trail, we go with the record and flag the discrepancy so you can see it.

### Step 3: We compute your match

For each candidate, we compare your positions to theirs across every relevant policy topic. Topics you said matter more to you count more in the match. The result is a percentage: 85% means you and the candidate agree on most of the things you care about. 40% means you disagree on more than you agree.

For ballot measures, we check whether voting YES or NO aligns with your stated positions on the topics the measure affects, and give you a plain-language recommendation with the reasoning behind it.

### Step 4: You make your own choices

We show you the match scores, the areas of agreement and disagreement, and the evidence behind each score. You can adjust your positions at any time — even while reviewing your ballot — and watch the recommendations update in real time.

You pick your votes. We don't pick them for you.

## How Candidate Scores Are Validated

### Multiple independent sources

We don't rely on a single source for any score. A candidate's climate score, for example, might be informed by their League of Conservation Voters rating, their votes on specific climate legislation, and their stated positions on their campaign website. When these sources agree, we have high confidence. When they don't, we flag it.

### Confidence levels

Every score carries a confidence rating:

- **High** — Multiple independent sources agree (voting record + scorecard + stated positions all point the same direction)
- **Medium** — One strong source or two consistent sources
- **Low** — Limited evidence available; the score is our best estimate but should be taken with more caution

If we don't have enough evidence to score a candidate on a particular topic, we leave it blank rather than guess.

### Evidence you can check

For each candidate comparison, you can see the specific evidence behind their scores — with links to the original sources where available. We show you the LCV score page, the bill text, the interview transcript. You don't have to take our word for it.

### What about new candidates with no record?

First-time candidates who have never held office don't have voting records or interest group ratings. In those cases, we rely on their stated positions from campaign materials and interviews. These scores carry lower confidence, and we tell you that.

## How Ballot Measure Recommendations Work

Each ballot measure is analyzed for which policy topics it affects and in what direction. For example, a rent control measure affects the "housing affordability" topic, pushing policy toward more government intervention.

We compare the measure's effects against your positions on those topics. If the measure pushes policy in the direction you prefer on the topics you care about, we recommend YES. If it pushes away from your positions, we recommend NO. If it's genuinely close, we tell you it's a close call and show you the breakdown.

If you shared your demographics (income, housing situation, age, etc.), we also show a "How this could affect you" section with personalized insights — for example, telling a renter how a housing measure might affect their costs, or telling a veteran which ID requirements apply to them.

## Your Privacy

- All your assessment answers, demographic data, and ballot selections are stored on your device only
- Nothing is sent to a server unless you explicitly submit feedback
- No account is required to use the tool
- We don't track your political positions or share them with anyone

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Ballot Builder affiliated with any political party or organization?**

No. The tool is designed to be nonpartisan. We score candidates on their individual records and positions, not their party membership. Our scoring methodology is documented and open to review.

**How do you keep political bias out of the scoring?**

We prioritize objective evidence (voting records, numerical ratings from established organizations) over subjective sources (news coverage, campaign rhetoric). We use interest group scorecards from across the political spectrum — both left-leaning organizations (like the League of Conservation Voters) and right-leaning ones (like the NRA and Chamber of Commerce).

We also apply a transparency principle: every score shows its sources, so you can evaluate the evidence yourself. Where evidence is thin or comes from only one side of the spectrum, we lower the confidence level.

We acknowledge that no scoring system is perfectly unbiased. Our methodology, evidence sources, and known limitations are documented and available for review.

**Why doesn't my candidate show a score on some topics?**

We only score topics where we have evidence. If a candidate hasn't taken a public position on housing policy and has no relevant votes, we leave that topic blank rather than filling in a guess. A missing score is more honest than a made-up one.

**Can I see exactly what evidence a score is based on?**

Yes. In the ballot view, tap "See value comparison" on any candidate to see the per-topic breakdown. Each topic shows the candidate's position and the sources behind it. Where available, evidence entries link to the original source (scorecard page, bill text, interview transcript).

**What if a candidate's position changed recently?**

We score their most recent position but flag the change. For example, if a candidate voted one way for a decade but took a different stance during the current campaign, you'll see both data points with a note explaining the discrepancy.

**How accurate are the match percentages?**

The match percentage reflects how closely your stated positions align with the candidate's documented positions on the topics you said matter to you. It is not a prediction of how you'll feel about the candidate overall — it doesn't account for personal character, leadership style, or single issues that might be deal-breakers for you.

Think of it as a starting point for your research, not a final answer.

**Does the system favor incumbents?**

Incumbents have more data available (voting records, scorecards), which means their scores tend to be more precise and carry higher confidence. Challengers and first-time candidates are scored on what evidence exists, with appropriately lower confidence levels. We don't give incumbents higher match scores — we just have more certainty about where they stand.

**What if I disagree with a score?**

You can adjust your own positions at any time in the ballot view — the recommendations update instantly. If you believe a candidate's score is wrong, we welcome that feedback. The tool is a prototype, and user scrutiny helps us improve accuracy.

**Does this work for every election?**

Currently, Ballot Builder covers select races in Michigan, North Carolina, Georgia, and Texas for the 2026 election cycle. We're working on expanding coverage. Not every race in these states is included — we prioritize contests where we can gather sufficient evidence to score candidates meaningfully.

**Where does my data go?**

Nowhere. Your assessment results, demographics, and ballot selections stay in your browser's local storage. If you clear your browser data, it's gone. A future version may offer optional account creation to save your data across devices, but that will always be opt-in.
