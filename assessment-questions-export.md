# Ballot Builder — Assessment Questions Export

17 policy axes used in the Civic Blueprint Assessment. Each question presents 4–6 position cards. The position marked **[Current Policy]** reflects the approximate U.S. status quo.

---

## Economic Domain

### 1. Safety Net Breadth (`econ_safetynet`)
**Question:** Should government help be available to more people with fewer requirements?
- Pole A: Broader Safety Net → Pole B: More Conditional Safety Net

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Universal support programs | Robust benefits available to all with minimal conditions |
| 2 | Broad eligibility with some conditions | Wide access to assistance with basic requirements |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Targeted programs with work incentives | Benefits for those in need with participation requirements |
| 4 | Strict eligibility and conditions | Aid limited to verified need with strong work requirements |
| 5 | Minimal safety net | Limited government assistance, emphasize self-reliance |

### 2. Public Investment (`econ_investment`)
**Question:** Should we pay more in taxes to fund public services?
- Pole A: More Public Investment → Pole B: Lower Taxes, Less Spending

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Major expansion of public services | Significantly increase spending on schools, infrastructure, and services |
| 2 | Targeted public investments | Increase funding for high-priority community needs |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Maintain current balance | Keep existing service and tax levels |
| 4 | Reduce spending, lower taxes | Cut programs to return money to taxpayers |
| 5 | Minimal government spending | Dramatically reduce taxes and public programs |

### 3. School Choice (`econ_school_choice`)
**Question:** Should education funding focus on public schools or follow student choice?
- Pole A: Strengthen Public Schools → Pole B: Expand School Choice

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Fund public schools exclusively | All education dollars go to neighborhood public schools |
| 2 | Prioritize public schools | Most funding to public schools with limited alternatives |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Mixed public and choice options | Public schools alongside charter and magnet programs |
| 4 | Expand school choice | Significant funding for charters, vouchers, and alternatives |
| 5 | Full funding portability | Families choose any school, funding follows the student |

### 4. Tax Structure (`econ_tax_structure`)
**Question:** How should the tax burden be distributed across income levels?
- Pole A: Progressive Taxation → Pole B: Flat / Sales Tax

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Highly progressive with wealth tax | Steep graduated rates plus taxes on large fortunes and capital gains |
| 2 | **[Current Policy]** Progressive income tax | Graduated rates where higher earners pay higher percentages |
| 3 | Simplified brackets with fewer deductions | Fewer tax brackets, lower top rate, close loopholes |
| 4 | Flat income tax | Everyone pays the same percentage regardless of income |
| 5 | Replace income tax with consumption tax | Eliminate income tax, fund government through sales or value-added tax |

## Healthcare Domain

### 5. Coverage Model (`health_coverage_model`)
**Question:** Should government offer health insurance to everyone?
- Pole A: More Government Insurance → Pole B: More Private Insurance

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Single-payer government health system | One public program replaces all private insurance |
| 2 | Public option available to all | Government plan competes alongside private insurance |
| 3 | Expand public programs (Medicare/Medicaid) | Lower Medicare age and broaden Medicaid eligibility |
| 4 | **[Current Policy]** Mix of public and private coverage | Medicare/Medicaid for some, employer plans for others |
| 5 | Private insurance with subsidies | Market-based coverage with tax credits for those who need help |
| 6 | Fully private insurance market | Individuals and families buy coverage directly from insurers |

### 6. Cost Control (`health_cost_control`)
**Question:** Should government set limits on healthcare prices?
- Pole A: Government Price Limits → Pole B: Market Competition

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Comprehensive price controls | Government sets all healthcare prices |
| 2 | Regulate prices in key areas | Negotiate drug prices, cap hospital charges |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Mix of regulation and competition | Some price rules plus transparency for shopping |
| 4 | Promote competition and transparency | Require price disclosure so consumers can compare |
| 5 | Let market set prices | Competition between providers drives efficiency |

### 7. Public Health (`health_public_health`)
**Question:** How should government approach public health and drug policy?
- Pole A: Prevention & Treatment → Pole B: Personal Choice & Enforcement

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Proactive public health programs | Government leads prevention, treatment, and harm reduction |
| 2 | Robust health education and services | Fund community health and treatment programs |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Balanced approach | Some public health programs alongside personal responsibility |
| 4 | Limited intervention | Focus on essential disease control only |
| 5 | Minimal government role | Leave health decisions to individuals, enforce drug laws |

## Housing Domain

### 8. Zoning & Supply (`housing_supply_zoning`)
**Question:** Should cities allow more housing to be built in existing neighborhoods?
- Pole A: Build More, Allow Density → Pole B: Preserve, Limit Growth

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Allow housing everywhere by right | Remove most zoning restrictions — any lot can have apartments |
| 2 | Allow density near transit and jobs | Upzone commercial corridors and transit stops, protect interior neighborhoods |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Case-by-case with community input | New development requires neighborhood review and approval |
| 4 | Preserve current neighborhood character | Maintain existing zoning to protect community stability |

### 9. Affordability Tools (`housing_affordability_tools`)
**Question:** Should government control rents and build public housing?
- Pole A: Rent Limits & Public Housing → Pole B: Build More, Fewer Rules

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Strong rent control and public housing | Cap rents and invest heavily in public/nonprofit housing |
| 2 | Rent stabilization with affordability requirements | Limit rent increases and require affordable units |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Mix of regulations and supply incentives | Some rent protections alongside encouraging construction |
| 4 | Focus on increasing supply | Build more housing, limit regulations that slow construction |
| 5 | Let the market work | Remove rent controls and mandates to maximize building |

### 10. Transit Priority (`housing_transport_priority`)
**Question:** Should cities invest more in transit or roads?
- Pole A: Transit & Biking → Pole B: Roads & Parking

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Prioritize transit, walking, and biking | Shift funding toward sustainable transportation |
| 2 | Major transit expansion | Grow bus and rail while maintaining roads |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Balanced investment | Fund transit, roads, and active transportation equally |
| 4 | Maintain roads with selective transit | Focus on road capacity, add transit where clearly needed |
| 5 | Prioritize roads and parking | Ensure drivers can get where they need to go |

## Justice Domain

### 11. Police Accountability (`justice_policing_accountability`)
**Question:** How much oversight should police have?
- Pole A: More Oversight & Alternatives → Pole B: More Police & Enforcement

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Strong independent oversight and alternatives | Civilian control and non-police crisis response |
| 2 | Civilian review with alternative responders | Oversight plus mental health teams for some calls |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Advisory oversight, co-responder model | Police and mental health work together |
| 4 | Support police with crisis training | More officers trained in crisis intervention |
| 5 | Expand police presence and authority | More officers with freedom to enforce proactively |

### 12. Sentencing Goals (`justice_sentencing_goals`)
**Question:** Should the justice system focus on rehabilitation or punishment?
- Pole A: Focus on Rehabilitation → Pole B: Focus on Punishment

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Rehabilitation and restoration | Focus on treatment, education, and reentry support |
| 2 | Shorter sentences with programming | Address root causes, invest in alternatives to prison |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Balance punishment and rehabilitation | Consequences plus programs for those who want them |
| 4 | Accountability with some programs | Clear punishment, programs available for motivated individuals |
| 5 | Strict punishment and longer sentences | Remove offenders to protect the public |

### 13. Firearms Policy (`justice_firearms`)
**Question:** How much regulation should there be on firearms?
- Pole A: Stronger Gun Safety Rules → Pole B: Fewer Restrictions

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Comprehensive licensing and registration | Mandatory training, licensing, and registration for all firearms |
| 2 | Ban assault-style weapons and high-capacity magazines | Prohibit semi-automatic rifles and magazines over 10 rounds |
| 3 | Universal background checks and red flag laws | Close private sale loopholes, allow courts to temporarily remove guns |
| 4 | **[Current Policy]** Current standards with state flexibility | Background checks for dealers, states set additional rules |
| 5 | Expand concealed carry and reduce restrictions | National concealed carry reciprocity, fewer purchase barriers |
| 6 | Constitutional carry, minimal regulation | No permits needed, few government barriers for law-abiding citizens |

### 14. Reproductive Rights (`justice_reproductive`)
**Question:** What should the legal status of abortion and reproductive healthcare be?
- Pole A: Full Reproductive Rights → Pole B: Protect Fetal Life

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Unrestricted access with public funding | Legal throughout pregnancy with government-funded coverage for all |
| 2 | Legal with viability limit (~24 weeks) | Broad access through viability with health exceptions after; reflects pre-Dobbs Roe standard |
| 3 | Legal with earlier gestational limit (12–15 weeks) | First-trimester access with exceptions for health and fetal anomalies after |
| 4 | **[Current Policy]** Post-Dobbs state patchwork | Each state sets its own rules; ranges from full bans to broad access |
| 5 | Exceptions only (rape, incest, life of mother) | General prohibition with narrow exceptions for extreme circumstances |
| 6 | Banned from conception, no public funding | Abortion prohibited at all stages; no government funding for reproductive services |

## Climate Domain

### 15. Ambition Level (`climate_ambition`)
**Question:** How quickly should we act on climate change?
- Pole A: Act Fast on Climate → Pole B: Go Slow, Keep Costs Low

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Emergency climate mobilization | Aggressive action even with significant short-term costs |
| 2 | Ambitious transition this decade | Major emissions cuts by 2035, net-zero by 2050 |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Steady transition balancing priorities | Meaningful progress while managing economic impacts |
| 4 | Gradual shift prioritizing affordability | Transition as clean energy becomes cost-competitive |
| 5 | Slow transition for stability | Avoid disrupting reliable, affordable energy |

### 16. Energy Portfolio (`climate_energy_portfolio`)
**Question:** What energy sources should we prioritize?
- Pole A: Solar & Wind First → Pole B: Mix of All Energy

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Renewables only | Phase out all fossil fuels, prioritize solar and wind |
| 2 | Primarily renewables with nuclear | Clean energy priority including nuclear as baseload power |
| 3 | **[Current Policy]** Diverse energy mix | Support renewables alongside existing energy sources |
| 4 | All-of-the-above including fossil fuels | Support domestic production across all energy types |
| 5 | Energy independence first | Maximize domestic fossil fuel production |

### 17. Permitting Speed (`climate_permitting`)
**Question:** How should we balance environmental review with project speed?
- Pole A: Thorough Review First → Pole B: Faster Approvals

| # | Position | Description |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Full environmental review for all projects | Comprehensive impact assessment before any construction |
| 2 | **[Current Policy]** Standard review with time limits | Complete assessment within 2 years, with community input |
| 3 | Fast-track clean energy and housing | Streamlined permitting for projects that reduce emissions or increase housing |
| 4 | Minimal review with basic safeguards | Approve most projects quickly, intervene only for clear harm |
